Welcome to **Stage 1: Foundations & Physical Media**. 

Before bits can become web pages, streaming video, or cloud database transactions, they must travel across the physical universe as modulated electrical voltages, laser photons through pure silica glass, and electromagnetic radio waves.

---

## Pedagogical Philosophy

<Callout type="info" title="The Physical Reality of Networking">
Software engineers often treat networks as magical, instantaneous pipes where data simply appears. But every network has physical constraints: the speed of light in glass ($\approx 200,000\text{ km/s}$), attenuation, electromagnetic noise, and cable length limits. Understanding the physical layer turns mysterious latency spikes and packet drops into predictable, debuggable physics.
</Callout>

In this foundational stage, you will learn:
* **The Evolution of Inter-Networking:** Why packet switching triumphed over circuit switching, how ARPANET was built, and how Tier 1 ISPs peer at Internet Exchange Points (IXPs).
* **Network Classifications & Topologies:** From Bluetooth PANs to multi-region WANs; from legacy bus topologies to modern datacenter Clos spine-and-leaf fabrics.
* **Transmission Hardware & Physics:** Twisted-pair copper cancellation (Cat5e through Cat8), optical fiber mechanics (Single-Mode vs. Multi-Mode), subsea ocean cables, and the speed of light.
