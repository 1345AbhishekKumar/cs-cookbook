Welcome to **Stage 4: Administration & Scaling** — where you stop being a database *user* and start being its *operator*.

Stages 1–3 taught you to design, query, and trust PostgreSQL. This stage is about running it: who is allowed to do what, how to size memory and connections, how to survive a server dying, and how to grow a single node into a reliable, replicated cluster without losing sleep.

---

## Pedagogical Philosophy

<Callout type="info" title="Least Privilege, then Capacity, then Redundancy">
Administration has a natural order: **secure it**, **size it**, then **multiply it**. Giving the app a superuser account "just to get it working" is the most common way small setups become breach write-ups. Fix access first, tune second, replicate third.
</Callout>

By the end of this stage you will be able to:

- Create roles and grant privileges precisely, and enforce per-row access with Row-Level Security.
- Understand the handful of `postgresql.conf` settings that actually matter, and pool connections with PgBouncer.
- Set up streaming replication, understand synchronous vs. asynchronous durability, and plan a failover.
- Install and reason about extensions, and safely perform a major-version upgrade.
