Welcome to **Stage 4: Distributed Data, Scaling & NoSQL** — where one machine becomes many.

A single PostgreSQL server can hold terabytes and serve thousands of transactions per second. Eventually, though, you hit a wall: more data than one disk, more traffic than one CPU, or a need to survive the loss of an entire data center. This stage is about the techniques — and the hard trade-offs — of spreading data across machines without corrupting it.

---

## Pedagogical Philosophy

<Callout type="info" title="Distribution Is a Trade, Not a Free Upgrade">
Every distributed technique buys one property by giving up another. Replication buys availability and read scaling, at the cost of consistency or write latency. Sharding buys write scalability, at the cost of cross-shard queries and transactions. There is no configuration that gives you everything — the **CAP theorem** formalizes exactly why. The skill is choosing *which* trade-off your workload can tolerate.
</Callout>

By the end of this stage you will be able to:

- Explain leader/follower, multi-leader, and leaderless replication, and the consistency each provides.
- Reason about replication lag, read-your-writes, and monotonic reads.
- Partition data by range or hash, use consistent hashing, and avoid hotspots.
- Distinguish CAP, PACELC, linearizability, and serializability without hand-waving.
- Describe two-phase commit and Raft consensus, and know when each matters.
- Choose among the NoSQL families — or justify staying relational.
