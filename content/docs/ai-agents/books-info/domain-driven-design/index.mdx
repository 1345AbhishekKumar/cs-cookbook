**Eric Evans · 2003**

<Callout type="info" title="For humans">
The origin of most of the vocabulary you now hear in architecture discussions — ubiquitous language, bounded
context, aggregate, anti-corruption layer, core domain. It is long and occasionally dense, and much of it is
about working with people rather than writing code. Read it once, properly, and the rest of the DDD shelf
becomes commentary. If you only have a weekend, read *Distilled* first and come back to this one.
</Callout>

## What it argues

When the business domain is genuinely complex, generic technical structure is not a neutral choice — it
quietly discards the knowledge the software exists to encode. The remedy is to make the **model** the centre
of design, and to make language do the work: a model that domain experts and developers can both speak
exposes contradictions early, when they are cheap. Because one language cannot mean the same thing
everywhere, models must be bounded, and the relationships between those boundaries must be designed
deliberately. Tactical patterns exist to protect model integrity, not to provide decoration.

## Core ideas

- **Ubiquitous language** — the vocabulary in the code and the vocabulary in the room must be the same one.
- **Model-driven design** — the model is not an analysis artefact; it is what the software is built from.
- **Model integrity** — protected invariants and explicit invariants beat well-meaning flexibility.
- **Bounded contexts** — the same word meaning two things is a signal to split, not to reconcile.
- **Distillation** — identify the core domain and protect it from the surrounding generic work.
- **Deep collaboration** — knowledge crunching happens with domain experts, continuously.
