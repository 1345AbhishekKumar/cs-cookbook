## What it argues

Technical debt is a metaphor with real interest payments: every smell you leave in place makes the next
change slower and riskier. Refactoring is the disciplined repayment plan — a loop of spotting a smell,
choosing the matching technique, applying it in small verified steps, and repeating. The material is
explicit that refactoring is not a rewrite: behaviour is preserved, the scope is controlled, and the work
happens continuously as part of development rather than as a heroic cleanup.

## Core ideas

- **Name the smell before fixing it** — a shared vocabulary makes review comments precise and actionable.
- **Match smell to technique** — bloaters, change preventers, dispensables, and couplers each have standard remedies.
- **Preserve behaviour, verify constantly** — tests run between steps; a broken test means undo, not debug forward.
- **Control refactoring scope** — refactor what this change touches, not the whole neighbourhood.
- **Small steps, always compiling** — the codebase should be shippable at every point in the sequence.
- **Refactor before and after the feature** — preparation makes the change easy; cleanup keeps the design honest.
