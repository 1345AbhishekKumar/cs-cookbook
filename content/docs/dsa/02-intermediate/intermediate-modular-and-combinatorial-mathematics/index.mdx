## The Necessity of Modular Arithmetic in Computer Science

Combinatorial counting problems (permutations, paths on grids, binary tree configurations) grow at hyper-exponential rates ($N!$, $2^N$, $\binom{2N}{N}$). For $N = 30$, $30! \approx 2.65 \times 10^{32}$, vastly exceeding the 64-bit unsigned integer maximum ($2^{64} - 1 \approx 1.84 \times 10^{19}$).

To prevent integer overflow while preserving algebraic properties, computing platforms and competitive programming benchmarks evaluate answers **modulo a large prime**, universally $M = 10^9 + 7$ ($1{,}000{,}000{,}007$) or $M = 998{,}244{,}353$.
